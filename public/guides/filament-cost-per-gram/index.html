Filament cost per gram and per meter

For a 1 kg spool, the cost per gram is simply the spool price divided by 1000: a 20 per kg spool costs 0.02 per gram, so a 50 g print uses 1.00 of filament. The catch is that slicers often report filament in meters, and how much a meter weighs depends on the material.

The formulas

  • Cost per gram = spool price ÷ grams of filament on the spool (not counting the spool itself).
  • Grams per meter (1.75 mm filament) = 2.405 × density in g/cm³. For 2.85 mm filament use 6.379 × density.
  • Cost of a print = grams used × cost per gram, plus an allowance for purge, failed prints and supports you throw away.

2.405 is the volume in cm³ of one meter of 1.75 mm filament: π × (0.0875 cm)² × 100 cm. The same for 2.85 mm gives 6.379.

What people forget

  • Waste. Purge towers, multi-color flushing, brims, supports and the occasional failed print are paid for too. A 5 to 10 percent allowance is a common starting point; multi-color prints can waste far more.
  • Spool weight. "1 kg" means 1 kg of filament; the empty spool weighs extra. Some cheaper spools hold less, such as 800 g, so divide by what is actually on it.
  • Shipping and tax on the filament belong in the price per kg.
