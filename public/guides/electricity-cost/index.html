How much electricity does a 3D print use?

Less than most people expect. A typical desktop printer averages about 80 to 110 watts while printing PLA, so a 10-hour print uses around 1 kWh. At the US average residential price that is about 18 to 20 cents. Materials that need a hot bed and chamber, such as ABS and PC, can double that.

The formula

Cost = average watts × print hours ÷ 1000 × price per kWh

Use the average draw while printing, not the power supply rating. A printer with a 350 W power supply rarely draws anything close to that once the bed is hot; the heaters cycle on and off. The warm-up at the start draws more, which the averages below already include.

Does electricity matter when pricing prints?

Usually a little. As an example, if a 10-hour PLA print uses 100 g of filament from a spool that cost 20 per kg, the material is 2.00 and electricity at 0.18 per kWh is about 0.19, roughly a tenth of the material. Machine wear, failed prints and your own time are usually bigger. It still belongs in the price: across hundreds of print hours a month it adds up, and it matters more for ABS, PC and large high-temperature printers.
