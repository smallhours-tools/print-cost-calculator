What does a 3D printer cost per hour to run?

Part of the cost of running a printer really does scale with time: electricity and machine wear. Filament doesn't. How many grams a printer lays down per hour varies more than tenfold between jobs, so an hourly rate on its own is a poor way to price prints. Here are both halves, with real numbers.

The machine: electricity plus wear per hour

  • Electricity = average watts ÷ 1000 × price per kWh. A printer averaging 100 W at the US average of 18.31¢ per kWh costs 1.8¢ per hour; at 0.15 per kWh (the calculator's default) it is 0.015. See electricity cost per print for measured watts by printer.
  • Wear = depreciation plus maintenance per hour. The calculator's placeholder is 0.15 per hour (a 400 printer over 4,000 hours, plus 0.05 maintenance); printer wear per hour shows how to work out your own.

With the calculator's defaults, the machine costs 0.165 per print hour, and wear is about ten times the electricity. If you only count electricity, you undercount the machine by roughly that factor.

The range is about 3.7 to 58.0 g per hour. The PrusaSlicer files for the Prusa MINI run from 7.6 to 13.4 g/h, the two long Cura files for UltiMaker printers are under 5 g/h, and the two-plate Bambu Studio job for an H2C deposits 58 g/h. At 20 per kg that is anywhere from about 0.07 to 1.16 of filament per hour, while the machine cost stays at 0.165.

So what is a fair hourly rate?

There isn't a single one, because filament dominates on fast, heavy prints and the machine dominates on slow, light ones. Price each print from both instead:

Cost = machine cost per hour × hours + grams × price per gram + your hands-on time

Then add margin and fees as in how much to charge for a 3D print. If you do want a rule of thumb for quoting before slicing, take the grams per hour of your own past jobs on that printer, not someone else's.
