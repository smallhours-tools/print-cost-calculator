How accurate is the 3D print cost calculator?

The calculator reads the print time and filament weight your slicer wrote into the file, so it is as accurate as the slicer's own estimate. To check that it reads them correctly, we sliced our own test models in Bambu Studio, CuraEngine, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer: 47 files. On all 41 files that state a weight, it reads the same print time (within 1 second) and weight (within 0.01 g) as the slicer. The 3 CuraEngine files state only filament length or volume, not weight: the calculator matches the print time on all 3, estimates the weight from PLA density and says so on the page. All 3 unsliced project files are refused with a message to slice them first.

A slicer's time is an estimate: firmware acceleration settings, pauses and filament changes can make the real print take longer or shorter. The slicer metadata guide shows where each slicer writes these numbers.

Slicers and settings

  • PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 (MK4S, Prusament PLA/PETG; XL 2T Input Shaper, Prusament PLA + PETG)
  • Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61 (P1S, Bambu PLA Basic / PETG HF; P1S + AMS PLA + PETG)
  • OrcaSlicer 2.4.2 (Creality K1C, Creality Generic PLA/PETG; two PLA colours)
  • CuraEngine 5.13.0 standalone (Creality Ender-3, UltiMaker S5; definitions only, see README)

The models are simple on purpose: a cube in PLA and PETG, a tiny cube, a part that needs supports, a vase-mode cylinder, a 21 to 28 hour slab, prints with two filaments and projects with two plates. The CuraEngine files come from Cura's slicing engine run on its own, so their numbers can differ from what the Cura app would give for the same model.

Also checked: files other people published

21 sliced files from public projects (PrusaSlicer's own test data, Cura, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio examples) were read and compared with the totals written in them, in October 2026. They are listed with their sources in reference-data.md; their licences don't let us copy them into the repository, so they aren't re-checked on every change.

Not covered yet

  • Files exported from the Cura app itself (only Cura's engine so far).
  • Slicers based on OrcaSlicer, such as Creality Print, ElegooSlicer, Anycubic Slicer Next, QIDI Studio and Snapmaker Orca. Their files usually look like OrcaSlicer's, but we haven't tested them.
  • Other slicers, such as Simplify3D, IdeaMaker and Kiri:Moto.
  • How close a slicer's time estimate is to the real print on your printer.
